About The Merit Company

A company can have a credible growth plan and still be organized in ways that make it harder to deliver. At critical inflection points, such as successive funding rounds, leadership changes or acquisitions, slower decisions and rising costs can signal that growth is outpacing the organization's readiness.

MCo calls this a growth wobble: when the leader, team, system or culture falls behind what the business is now expected to deliver.

MCo assesses those four connected forces against the growth plan or investment case, identifying what needs to change and why it matters commercially. Senior operators then lead the agreed work alongside the client's team, with MCo accountable from diagnosis through implementation.

The firm brings together senior operators across strategy, operations and human capital, combining executive experience inside businesses with backgrounds in leading management consulting and investment firms.

Signature talk samples

The Growth Wobble

What to change when your business outgrows how it works.

The numbers can still look good while the organization is beginning to struggle. Decisions may take longer, coordination becomes more expensive and capable leaders find that what worked before no longer produces the same result.

Robin calls this a growth wobble: the gap that emerges when the leader, team, system or culture falls behind what the business now requires. She explores how these four forces interact, why the apparent people problem may have a different cause, and how to decide what needs to change before the consequences become harder to reverse.

The Intersection of AI and Human Cognition

How leaders distinguish convincing output from sound judgment.

AI can help someone produce an impressive answer faster than they develop the judgment to evaluate it. That creates a different challenge for leaders: understanding what people know, where they need to exercise judgment and who remains accountable for the result.

Robin examines what this means for how companies assess talent and organize work. She connects the human questions to the commercial ones, including why time saved does not automatically become value created.

The Leadership Assumptions Behind the Investment Case

Assessing whether the team is equipped to deliver what the investment requires.

An investment case makes assumptions about the organization's ability to execute. An acquisition strategy assumes the team can integrate businesses. A margin target may require executives to manage greater complexity without adding cost at the same rate. A strong track record does not necessarily establish readiness for those demands.

Robin examines how investors can assess leadership execution risk during diligence and revisit those judgments as the company enters its next stage. She explores how to distinguish a leadership capability gap from an operating problem, and what needs to change for the team to deliver the value-creation plan. Where success depends on an executive developing new capabilities, that learning curve has implications for timing and performance expectations.
